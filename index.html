Semantic Field

Semantic field: a group of words connected by a shared idea or concept.

By constructing a semantic field of [idea] with the terms '[word 1]', '[word 2]' and '[word 3]', (author) underscores ..., positioning ...

EXAMPLE:

By constructing a semantic field of destruction with the terms ‘'damaged', 'shattered' and 'broken', Wargan cumulatively underscores the ominous repercussions of retail crime, positioning local shoppers to reject passive bystanding.

Auxesis

Auxesis: the deliberate arrangement of words or ideas in increasingly forceful order.

Auxetically escalating from '[least intense]' to '[more intense]' and finally '[most intense]', (author) progressively intensifies ..., positioning ...

EXAMPLE:

Auxetically escalating from ‘uncomfortable’ to ‘unsafe’ and finally ‘dangerous’, Wargan progressively intensifies the consequences of unchecked retail crime, positioning local shoppers to fear that continued passivity will progressively threaten their community.

Syntactic Parallelism

Syntactic parallelism refers to the repetition of a word at the beginning of 2 or more consecutive sentences.

Syntactically parallelising the repeated opening '[word]' in '[sentence 1]' and '[sentence 2]', (author) repetitiously underscores ..., positioning ...

EXAMPLE:

Syntactically parallelising the repeated opening ‘we’ in ‘We report the incident. We support the worker’ and ‘We must protect the community’, Wargan repetitiously underscores the collective responsibility of Roxburgh Park residents, creating rhythmic insistence that mobilise them to view safe intervention as a shared duty.
